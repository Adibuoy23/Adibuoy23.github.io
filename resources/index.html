    Dissertation¶

    Visual Time — PhD thesis, Department of Psychological & Brain Sciences, Johns Hopkins University, 2021. Advisor: Jonathan Flombaum.

    The thesis asks what a moment of visual experience is made of, and how such moments accumulate into a sense of duration. See How is information perceived? for the work that came out of it.

    Read the dissertation

    Experiment demos¶

    Playable, in-browser versions of the paradigms used in my research. They run best on a desktop browser in a full-screen window — several of them time stimuli precisely, so a phone or a small window will not reproduce the real experiment.

    Storyboard paradigm¶

    Watch a short film clip, then drag the frames that best tell its story onto a storyboard. This is how key moments are identified — and people agree strikingly well on which frames matter.

    Try the storyboard paradigm

    Used in Meaningful moments during film viewing are represented and remembered similarly across participants (preprint). See How is information remembered? for what came out of it.

    Event segmentation paradigm¶

    Watch a film and press a key whenever you feel one meaningful unit of activity has ended and another has begun. The classic event-boundary task, and the comparison against which key moments are measured.

    Try the event segmentation paradigm

    Used in Key moments and event boundaries in film comprehension and memory (preprint · data & code).

    Spatiotemporal disruptions during film viewing¶

    A film plays with brief flickers, and occasionally jumps forward or backward in time across one. Can you tell when it skipped? Most people miss a good number of them — and miss the forward jumps more often.

    Try the flicker paradigm

    Used in Spatiotemporal jump detections during continuous film viewing: Insights from a flicker paradigm (paper · data). See How is information perceived? for background.
